# References

Peer-reviewed sources retrieved from PubMed (metadata verified there). Each entry says what it supports in this project — and what it does not — so the list is not read as stronger evidence than it is.

| # | Reference | What it supports here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Michiels S, Koscielny S, Hill C. **Prediction of cancer outcome with microarrays: a multiple random validation strategy.** *Lancet* 2005;365:488–492. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(05)17866-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(05)17866-0) | Re-analysing seven published prognostic gene-expression studies, the gene lists were highly unstable across training sets, published results were over-optimistic, and five of seven did not classify better than chance. The authors advocate **validation by repeated random sampling** — what we do with 30 splits. |
| 2 | Ambroise C, McLachlan GJ. **Selection bias in gene extraction on the basis of microarray gene-expression data.** *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA* 2002;99:6562–6566. [doi:10.1073/pnas.102102699](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.102102699) | Error estimates are biased when genes are selected using the same samples that are later tested; **gene selection must happen inside the training data** at every validation step. This is why every selection step here uses training patients only. |
| 3 | Simon R, Radmacher MD, Dobbin K, McShane LM. **Pitfalls in the use of DNA microarray data for diagnostic and prognostic classification.** *J Natl Cancer Inst* 2003;95:14–18. [doi:10.1093/jnci/95.1.14](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/95.1.14) | A review of common design pitfalls in this kind of study. (The abstract was not available from PubMed, so it is cited for its topic only.) |
| 4 | Harrell FE, Lee KL, Mark DB. **Multivariable prognostic models: issues in developing models, evaluating assumptions and adequacy, and measuring and reducing errors.** *Stat Med* 1996;15:361–387. [doi:10.1002/(SICI)1097-0258(19960229)15:4<361::AID-SIM168>3.0.CO;2-4](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1097-0258(19960229)15:4%3C361::AID-SIM168%3E3.0.CO;2-4) | Describes an easily interpretable index of predictive discrimination for censored survival data (the **C-index**), warns about over-fitted models and recommends unbiased validation by bootstrapping or cross-validation. |
| 5 | Venet D, Dumont JE, Detours V. **Most random gene expression signatures are significantly associated with breast cancer outcome.** *PLoS Comput Biol* 2011;7:e1002240. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002240](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002240) | In breast cancer, 60 % of 47 published outcome signatures were not significantly better than random gene sets of the same size, and most random signatures were significantly associated with outcome, largely because of proliferation. This motivates our **comparison against random signatures**. (The study is on breast cancer, not LUAD; we cite it for the statistical pitfall, not as a LUAD result.) |
| 6 | McShane LM, Hayes DF. **Publication of tumor marker research results: the necessity for complete and transparent reporting.** *J Clin Oncol* 2012;30:4223–4232. [doi:10.1200/JCO.2012.42.6858](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2012.42.6858) | Argues for complete and transparent reporting of prognostic marker studies (the REMARK criteria), including design, analysis and results. We report the cohort, all split results and the unfavourable findings. |
| 7 | Collins GS, Reitsma JB, Altman DG, Moons KGM. **Transparent reporting of a multivariable prediction model for individual prognosis or diagnosis (TRIPOD): the TRIPOD statement.** *Ann Intern Med* 2015;162:55–63. [doi:10.7326/M14-0697](https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-0697) | A 22-item checklist for reporting prediction-model studies; used as a guide for what to report (data, split, performance, limitations). This repository is not a formal TRIPOD submission. |
| 8 | Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network. **Comprehensive molecular profiling of lung adenocarcinoma.** *Nature* 2014;511:543–550. [doi:10.1038/nature13385](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13385) | The origin of the TCGA-LUAD cohort. That paper profiled 230 tumours; the Xena/GDC release used here is larger (474 patients met our inclusion criteria). |

## What the literature does *not* say

- None of these sources prescribes the **size of a signature** (we use 15 genes, inherited from the first version of the project and not tuned) or compares our exact scoring rule.
- The **sign-aware score** (each gene counted in the direction of its effect) is our own design choice, justified by simple reasoning and tested on synthetic data, not by a specific paper.
- The TCGA barcode convention used to keep primary tumours only (sample type `01`; `11` is matched normal tissue) is standard TCGA documentation and was not retrieved from PubMed.
